## Design rules (decided here)
- **Error bound, not taste:** a coarse level may only be shown where its geometric error is at most 1 screen pixel. Each level records its simplification tolerance (metres) in `tiles.json`; the client picks the finest level whose tolerance is above nothing it needs, i.e. it uses a coarse level only when metres per pixel >= that level's tolerance, with hysteresis so it does not flicker.
- What a coarse level keeps or drops (for example small buildings, trees, markings, footways, service roads) and how far it merges (for example 1 km and 4 km blocks) is your proposal, chosen from measurements and written in an ADR. Heights and positions stay real: simplification may only remove detail or reduce vertices within the tolerance; it never moves or invents features.
- Switching levels never leaves a hole: a tile keeps drawing until what replaces it has loaded; a parent and its children are never drawn together over the same ground.
- Buildings in coarse tiles keep their feature keys, so the card still opens on a click at any zoom (the card shows the same data as at full detail).

## Constraints
- Budgets (`VERIFICATION.md` 5): <= 300 draw calls and <= 250 KB gzip per tile in every measured view; report any view above. Do not lower a budget.
- Deterministic pipeline; no new dependencies; no edits to the palette, heights rules, ranking or the band layout (ADR-0034).
- The visual baselines must not change at views where the base level is used. If a baseline view (`visual.spec.ts`, for example `wide` at 1200 m on 412x915) switches to a coarse level by the rule above, do not update the baseline: stop and report with both images.
- Do not run formatters that rewrite whole files; keep diffs minimal. Never leave a server or process running. Use `E2E_PORT=4187`.
- Commit messages end with `Co-Authored-By: Claude Sonnet 5.5 <noreply@anthropic.com>`.
