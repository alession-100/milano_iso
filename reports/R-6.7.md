## The rule (ADR-0043)
0. Sampling every 7 s; the run fails unless the sampled widths reach 300 m or less and 3,800 m or more.
1. Heap (minus shards loaded x 0.06 MB), external memory, renderer-process memory: last-minute median at most +5 / +25 / +20 % over the first judged minute AND fitted slope at most 0.5 / 0.5 / 6 MB/min (12 MB/min for runs under 10 minutes). Warm-up 60 s (180 s for the renderer process).
2. Renderer geometries and textures never above what the app holds (textures 0; geometries at most 1 and in at most 5 % of samples) and under the cache bound.
3. Tiles in scene at most max(64, peakWanted); DOM nodes constant; programs at most 8 and unchanged in the last minute.

## Attribution of the renderer-process growth (not resolved)
- Excluded by measurement: JS heap (adjusted slope -0.05 MB/min over 20 min), ArrayBuffer backing stores (flat, 4 to 13 MB), GPU process (246 to 330 MB, 0.9 MB/min), geometries, textures, programs and DOM nodes (bounded or constant), the shard catalogue (2.5 MB in all), and new content: in the closed-path run the path repeats every 100 s and shards stayed at 51 to 52 from minute 2, yet renderer memory went 359 to 398 MB over minutes 4 to 15.
- Shape: 183, 267, 348 MB in minutes 1 to 3, then 357 to 408 MB over minutes 4 to 20 (fitted 3.4 MB/min; about 2.3 MB/min over the last 8 minutes): slowing, but not flat at 20 minutes. The earlier 12-minute run (280 to 457 MB) is consistent.
- Not done: a native-memory breakdown (CDP exposes none) or heap-snapshot comparison (the V8 heap is not what grows). The likely class is native allocator growth in the renderer (command buffers, decoded-data pools, fragmentation from tile churn), but that is a guess, not shown.
- Escalate condition 1 of the card applies. The rule passes with a 6 MB/min limit, so it would not catch a process-memory leak below that.

## Catalogue decision
Kept and accounted for, not bounded. Probe (`catalogue_probe.json`, headless; the app's `{...tile, level}` copies in a per-level list and a map by id): all 101 shards 2.53 MB, mean 0.025 MB, largest shard 0.068 MB. The rule subtracts shards x 0.06 MB. This is lower than the card's 7.5 to 12.4 MB heap figure (4 to 88 shards); I did not reconcile the two. In the 20-minute run raw heap went 9.9 to 12.0 MB while shards went 24 to 78 and adjusted heap went 8.5 to 7.3 MB (the 0.06 allowance is a little high, which only loosens the heap rule by under 1 MB). Bounding would need eviction in `lod.ts` (T-6.6 territory) for at most 2.5 MB. No eviction test was needed.

## Deviations and assumptions
- ADR number 0043, not 0042, to leave 0042 to T-6.6, which runs in parallel.
- The thresholds were tuned after the first 5-minute run and the 20-minute run, from their data (renderer warm-up 180 s, 12 MB/min for short runs, external +25 %, one transient orphan geometry allowed). They are above the clean runs and far under the leak's. Run 1 and the 20-minute and closed-path runs were re-judged offline, not re-run.
- A first headed attempt was starved by other heavy processes on the machine and was killed (its output discarded); the per-sample PowerShell start (about 20 s then) was replaced by one long-lived helper.
- The short-run renderer slope limit is looser than the long-run one because only two minutes follow the warm-up.
- The shard probe is headless; the sessions are headed. The 15-minute closed-path run is an extra for attribution.
- The Linux and macOS process-memory branches are written but not run.
- A transient orphan geometry (+1) appeared in 1 of 42 samples of run 1 and 1 of 160 of the 20-minute run; cause not identified.
- Process memory is the sum over all `renderer`-type processes of the browser.

## Open questions and risks
- Renderer-process memory still rises about 2 to 3 MB/min after minute 3 and is not flat at 20 minutes. A longer run (an hour) or a native-memory dump (`chrome://tracing` memory-infra) is needed to tell a plateau from a slow leak. Owner decision.
