## Finding (the card's premise is wrong)
The card says the overestimate is the shadow pass over a ring of tiles and that the main pass is "as now". I implemented the card's change (shadow casters counted over the sun's shadow frustum) and measured the plan against the real count split by pass at 1440x900, origin, 600 m, budget switch disabled (`?budget=off`):

| Pass | Plan | Real | Plan - real |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Main pass (`?shadows=0`) | 231 | 155 | **76** |
| Shadow pass (total minus main) | 58 | 49 | 9 |
| Total | 289 | 204 | 85 |

The shadow plan is within 9 after the change (before: the ring). The remaining overcount is the **main pass**: the plan counts every layer of every tile whose content bounding sphere meets the camera frustum (30 tiles, 231 meshes); three.js culls each layer mesh by its own, smaller bounding sphere and draws 154 of them (probe in page: tile-sphere plan 231, per-mesh test 154, matches the 155 real). The edge tiles of the view contribute most of the gap: a layer mesh that covers only part of the tile is culled although the tile's sphere is in view. The manifest has only the tile's content box and a feature count per layer, no per-layer extents, so no manifest-only plan can be both tight and never below real (a tile box test undercounts where a mesh sphere pokes out of the box corners). A tight plan needs per-layer extents from the tile files (pipeline change, forbidden) or the real bounding spheres of the loaded meshes (client change in what the plan reads, with a load-behaviour question, below).

## Open questions and risks (decision for the orchestrator)
Proposed design that I did not try: **measured plan**. For a tile that is loaded, count its actual meshes against the camera frustum and its casters against the shadow frustum (exact); for a tile not loaded yet, keep the conservative tile-sphere count. Plan stays at or above real, and is exact once the view is settled (which is what the budget covers). Costs and open points:
1. At the first frame the level-0 plan is still over the cap (nothing loaded), so the controller must treat "over the cap with unloaded tiles" as unknown and keep requesting the base level, else level 0 never loads (chicken and egg). That loads level-0 tiles that are then dropped in the band where level 0 truly is over the cap (about 900 m at 1920 px): extra bytes and a brief level-0 flash. A bound on when to probe is needed (for example only when the conservative plan is under about 1.5 times the cap).
2. The notch 600 to 630 to 600 m would end on level 0 only while the level-0 tiles of the view stay loaded.
3. The plan then depends on streaming state, which weakens the unit-test determinism of `chooseBudgetLevel`; tests need loaded-mesh fixtures.
The alternative is a pipeline change (per-layer bounds in the tile entries, `counts` next to a `bounds` field), which this card forbids.
