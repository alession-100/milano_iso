## Design choices
- **Instance data in the feature table JSON, not the glb.** The table must expose every tree's fields anyway; adding `x`, `z` there avoids a second copy of the positions in the glb and keeps the glb a pure mesh file. Each tile builds one `InstancedMesh` (trunk + cone crown merged into one geometry with vertex colours, shared by all tiles) = 1 draw call per tile. Trees do not cast shadows (style choice, keeps shadow pass cheap).
- Crossings: the extract supports both `footway=crossing` ways (245 in the pilot) and `highway=crossing` nodes (283). Ways are used (they have the direction); nodes are not used. Only `crossing` in marked/traffic_signals/uncontrolled/zebra get stripes.
- Tag rules live in `decor.py`, not in `classify.RULES` (see deviations).

## Deviations and assumptions
1. Tree and crossing tag rules are in `decor.py`, not `classify.RULES`: adding layers there would change `normalise` outputs (layers, completeness and extent assertions, committed T-1.3 artifacts) and is outside this card. They read the OSM interim extract directly.
2. Style choices not fixed by the card (all in `style.py`): fill grid 12 m, jitter 3.5 m, seed 4101, clearances (building 2, road 1 from the ribbon edge, road area 1, water 1.5, trees 4 m), edge inset 1.5 m, default height 8 m with +/-25% seeded jitter for fill, centre-line classes (primary, secondary, tertiary, trunk, unclassified, residential), dash 3 m / gap 4.5 m / 0.2 m wide, 5 m end trim, zebra stripe 0.5 m wide at 1 m pitch, 3 m long. Including `residential` is my choice (it gives most dashes); drop it from `CENTRE_LINE_CLASSES` if the owner finds it too busy.
