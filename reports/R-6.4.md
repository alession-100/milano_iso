## Tuning (every change with its before and after)
All "before" numbers are from the build of commit 579174b (the bench and the city default, no tuning), measured in an earlier session on the same machine; "after" from the final build.

| # | change | measured effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Loaded tiles never move: local matrices computed once and frozen (`freezeMatrices`), and `matrixAutoUpdate = false` on the scene and the tile root. Found in the profile: `compose` (1.93 % of samples), `updateMatrix` (1.04), `multiplyMatrices` (0.96) and part of `updateMatrixWorld` (1.66) ran for every mesh of every tile every frame. Freezing only the tiles was not enough: the root's own `updateMatrix` marked its world matrix dirty each render, so three recomputed every descendant (`multiplyMatrices` rose to 2.97 %); freezing the roots removed it | main-thread busy time over the 80 s path (profile, non-minified, all other things equal): 36,779 ms, then 35,997 ms (tiles only), then 35,298 ms (tiles and roots); `compose` and `updateMatrix` leave the top 30 |
| 2 | Allocation-free per-frame work: the tile culling uses one scratch `Box3` (it made a `Box3` and two `Vector3` per tile per camera change; `boxOf` was 0.81 % of samples), the camera change test compares 32 numbers instead of building a string from them, the sun shadow frustum is refitted only when the camera or the bounds changed, the minimap polygon is written only when its points changed | not measured separately (only in combination with 1 and 3); `boxOf` is not in the top 16 of the profile afterwards |
| 3 | ADR-0039: no building shadows from level 3 on (a look change at the coarsest level, base level unchanged). In the profile the shadow pass drew every building a second time: probe at the origin, 4,000 m, shadows off against on: 744,981 against 1,537,540 triangles, 133 against 168 draw calls | desktop level-3 segments: max draw calls 211 and 239 become 164 and 179, max triangles 1,869,308 and 2,033,726 become 843,197 and 893,473; **phone-shaped 412 x 915 window: 318 draw calls and 2,247,546 triangles (over the 300 budget) become 257 and 1,571,937**. The desktop maximum (290, at level 2) is unchanged because level 2 keeps its shadows |
| all | combined, no display cap | 205.4 and 207.1 fps mean (CPU 4.05 and 4.01 ms) become 225.9 and 227.2 (3.68 and 3.66 ms): +10 % frame rate, -9 % CPU per frame |

The profile shows no single dominant cost: with the display at 120 Hz the main thread is idle 56 % of the time, and the busy part is spread over three.js' render loop (state changes, `bindVertexArray`, uniforms, program selection, `projectObject`), which scales with the number of draw calls. I did not find a per-frame waste beyond items 1 and 2. What would cut the draw calls further is fewer meshes per tile (merging layers in the pipeline), which is out of scope here.

**Before and after images** (level 3, 4,000 m, day, headed Chrome, `?e2e=1`): `reports/artifacts/T-6.4/shadows-before/` and `shadows-after/` (`l3-centre`, `l3-north-west`, `l2-centre`, at 1280 x 720 and 412 x 915; `l2-centre` at 2,000 m is level 2 on the desktop and is identical before and after). The difference is the soft darkening beside tall buildings at level 3; nothing else changes. The base-level visual baselines are untouched (e2e below).

## Memory session (5 minutes, twice)
`npm --prefix web run memory -- --port 4191 --out reports/artifacts/T-6.4/memory_run{1,2}.json` (not part of the default e2e; script `web/scripts/memory-session.mjs`). Headed Chrome, real GPU, `?e2e=1` (the renderer counts are test state; this turns MSAA off, which does not change what is held). The camera follows a fixed Lissajous over the whole city and sweeps 250 m to 4,000 m wide every 50 s (levels 0 to 3), and every 10 s one real wheel turn and one real mouse drag are sent. Every 10 s: forced GC (`HeapProfiler.collectGarbage`), then `Runtime.getHeapUsage`, `renderer.info.memory` (geometries, textures), programs, tiles loaded and in the scene, DOM nodes. 30 samples per run (`memory_summary.md` has every sample of both runs).

**Pass rule** (samples after the first minute, which is the warm-up):
1. heap: the median of the last minute (t 250 to 300 s) is within 10 % of the median of the second minute (t 70 to 120 s);
2. geometries and textures never exceed what the tile cache allows: the renderer holds a geometry only for a tile that is in the scene, so (renderer geometries minus geometries of the tiles in the scene) <= 1 (the ground plane), (renderer textures minus the tiles' textures) <= 0, and geometries <= max(cache limit 64, peak wanted tiles) x (largest geometry count of a tile) + 1;
3. tiles in the scene <= max(cache limit 64, the largest wanted set of the session): a wanted tile is never evicted (ADR-0038).

| | run 1 | run 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| heap median minute 2 / last minute | 11.80 / 11.92 MB | 11.81 / 12.01 MB |
| heap change | +1.05 % | +1.64 % |
| max geometries (bound) | 310 (691) | 310 (691) |
| renderer minus tile geometries / textures, max | -37 / -64 | -37 / -64 |
| max tiles in the scene (limit 64; peak wanted 69) | 67 | 67 |
| programs after minute 1 | 5 to 6 | 5 to 6 |
| DOM nodes after minute 1 | 74 (constant) | 74 (constant) |
| **pass** | **yes** | **yes** |

The heap is the JS heap (about 12 MB): geometry data lives in ArrayBuffers and on the GPU, which the heap figure does not include; the geometry and texture counts are what bound those (every evicted tile is disposed: the renderer count stays below the tile count at every sample). There was no growth to attribute.

## Owner steps: frame rate on the Pixel 7a and on the desktop
Needs the built site and the city tiles (they are not in git): in the repository, with `MILANO_ISO_DATA` pointing at the data directory: `make web-tiles EXTENT=city && make web-tiles EXTENT=pilot`, then `npm --prefix web run build:prod` (the first run compresses 340 MB of tiles and takes about 15 minutes; later builds about 4).
1. **Start the server for the network:** `npm --prefix web run serve -- --host 0.0.0.0 --port 4174`. It prints a placeholder address; the real one is the IPv4 address of the PC's Wi-Fi adapter (`ipconfig`). Windows may ask to allow Node on the private network: allow it. While it runs, anyone on that network can read the site; stop it with Ctrl+C when done. (`.claude/launch.json` now also has a `web-serve` configuration for the plain local server on 4174.)
2. **Phone** (Pixel 7a, on the same Wi-Fi, Chrome, screen kept on, battery saver off): open `http://<PC IPv4 address>:4174/?debug=bench`. The city opens by default. The benchmark starts by itself: first a hold at the start view until the tiles are in (up to 60 s), then 80 s of fixed path; do not touch the screen. The debug overlay (bottom of the screen) is collapsed to its title bar on a phone; **when the run is over it opens by itself** (or tap the title "Debug" to open or close it).
3. **What to read:** line 1 `fps mean N  1% low N  p5 N` (this is the number to compare with the target: **mean >= 30**; 1 % low shows stutter); `max draw calls N` (<= 300); `max triangles`; and one line per segment (`name: fps, 1% low, calls, tris, L<level>`), which shows where it is slowest. Write down the mean, the lowest segment fps, the max draw calls, and the `dpr` line of the live readout (tap the title before the run to see it: `dpr 1 (device 2.625)` means the page draws at 1 pixel per CSS pixel). The phone's display refresh rate (60, 90 or 120 Hz) caps the mean. Report also whether the map looks right (nothing missing at the coarse levels).
4. **Desktop:** the same URL on the PC, `http://127.0.0.1:4174/?debug=bench` in the browser you normally use; same readout. The pilot, for comparison with the 118.3 fps of 2026-11-17: add `&tiles=tiles/pilot/`.
5. Stop the server (Ctrl+C in its window).

## Deviations and assumptions
1. **Headed browser:** Chrome 157 (installed), not the bundled Chromium (cannot start headed here). The numbers are for that browser and the Intel GPU it picked.
2. **Display cap:** the frame rate reached the display refresh rate (119.7 before, 101.7 after, same machine, different session), so the before and after mean frame rates are not comparable; the uncapped runs and the CPU time are. I could not find out why the refresh differed.
3. **No Chromium timeline trace:** the card asks for a Chromium performance trace; I used a V8 sampling profile (CDP `Profiler`) plus the per-frame bench numbers. `bench-city.mjs --trace` writes a trace but I did not use it.
4. **ADR-0039 is a look change at level 3** (no building shadows), outside what the desktop budgets strictly needed (they held before). I made it because the phone-shaped window went over the draw-call budget (318) and the shadow pass was half the triangles. Images are in the report. It is easy to revert (`NO_SHADOW_FROM_LEVEL`). Needs your decision at G6 review.
5. **Memory rule corrected twice during the work, the heap threshold untouched.** Three pairs of sessions were run. The first pair (kept in `reports/artifacts/T-6.4/preliminary/`, heap +1.68 % and +2.76 %) was scored against a cache rule I had written badly: it counted a tile's textures as zero (my counter missed the per-tile lookup textures kept in `userData`) and compared the tiles in the scene with 64, although a wanted tile is never evicted. I corrected the rules and re-ran: the second pair (heap +0.54 % and +1.28 %; JSON not kept, overwritten) passed in run 1 but run 2 failed the tile rule by sampling (67 tiles in the scene against a sampled wanted maximum of 66: the cache evicts only when a tile arrives, so it can hold more than a later, smaller wanted set). I then recorded the session peak of the wanted set (`peakWanted`) in the test state and ran the third pair, the final one in `memory_run{1,2}.json` (+1.05 % and +1.64 %), which passes under the rule stated above. The 10 % heap rule is the card's and did not change; all six sessions had heap growth under 3 %.
6. **Heap figure excludes ArrayBuffer and GPU memory** (about 12 MB of JS heap); the geometry and texture counts bound those. I did not measure process memory.
7. **e2e pinning:** 30 `goto` lines now carry `&tiles=tiles/pilot/` (the fixture is served for `/tiles/pilot/`); the card said the fixture setup is unchanged, and it is (no route changed), but the tests' URLs did change.
8. **Evidence scripts** from earlier tasks (`capture-t5x.mjs`, `measure-real.mjs`, `measure-landmarks.mjs`, `measure-pick-real.mjs`, `measure-contrast.mjs`) open the default tileset and now open the city, not the pilot, unless given `tiles=tiles/pilot/`. I did not change them (not part of any suite).
9. **`serve.mjs --host`** is new (default stays 127.0.0.1, ADR-0038 point 4); needed so a phone can reach it. `payload/measurements.md` keeps the heading "T-6.3 measurements" from the script.
10. **Debug overlay:** the bench has a warm-up (untimed) and the readout updates 4 times a second during a run; both change what the bench measures a little compared with the 10 s pilot bench of T-3.4 (that path and its test still work). The overlay opens when a run ends on a small screen.
11. Time to first render and payload were measured with software GL and a throttled network (as in T-6.3), not with the real GPU.

## Open questions and risks
- **Draw-call margin at level 2 is 10 (3 %)** at 1280 x 720 (290); a differently shaped window at the level-2 widths could cross 300 (412 x 915 gives 241 there). Setting `NO_SHADOW_FROM_LEVEL = 2` would cut it, with a visible change at level 2; I did not do that.
- The Pixel 7a result is open; it is the one budget not measured. At the phone's shape the app draws up to 257 calls and 1.57 M triangles at level 1 to 3, which the Mali GPU has not been tried with.
- With the display at 120 Hz the desktop mean was 119.7 and the 1 % low about 95 to 97 fps before the tuning, and 88.5 now at 101.7 Hz; the 1 % low is mostly tile uploads at level changes (the unlimited runs show a 1 % low of about 43 fps, i.e. 23 ms frames when tiles arrive). If stutter at level changes matters, uploading tiles over several frames is the next step.
